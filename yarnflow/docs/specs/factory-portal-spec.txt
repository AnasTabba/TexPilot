   0. Instructions to the AI builder
   You are building the Factory Portal of a textile factory management system. Follow these
   rules strictly:

    1. Implement only what is written in this document. Do not add features, roles, screens,
       fields or integrations that are not listed. If something is unclear, ask before building. Do
       not guess.
    2. Items marked [ASSUMPTION] are working assumptions. Implement them as written
       but keep them easy to change.
    3. This is the factory-side portal only. The Supplier portal and the Buyer portal are
       separate applications. Do not build their screens. Build only the factory-side interfaces
       and data they need (see section 9).
    4. Enforce permissions on the backend (role-based access control). Hiding buttons in the
       UI is not enough.
    5. Every decision, approval, rejection, override and threshold change must be written to
       an audit log.
    6. Any rejection, dismissal, override or intervention requires a reason. The system must
       refuse to save it without one.
    7. Keep the API key for the shipment tracking service on the server only. Never expose it
       to the front end.

   2. Global rules

   2.1 Authority chain
        Quality Inspector → Supervisor → Admin.
        The supervisor decides routine items below a threshold. Above the threshold, the item
        waits for admin approval.
        Admin can see every action by every role and can intervene on any item at any time. An
        intervention must include a reason and is logged.
        A lower role cannot override a higher role.


   2.2 Admin-configured thresholds (four, each separate, set only by admin)
    1. Purchase threshold: compared against the total value of the accepted bid / PO (not
       per-item).
    2. Inventory adjustment threshold: inventory corrections and write-offs.
    3. Claim threshold: confirmed lot rejections and claims.
    4. Dispatch stock issue threshold: stock issues raised by dispatch.

   Below the threshold: the acting role's decision is final. At or above it: the item stays in a
   "pending admin approval" state and takes no effect until admin approves. Admin rejection
   requires a reason. Every threshold change is logged with who changed it, old value, new
   value and timestamp.


   2.3 Audit log
   Record for every action: user, role, action, object type and id, old and new values where
   relevant, reason (where required), timestamp. Admin can view the full log. The log cannot
   be edited or deleted.

   2.4 Notifications
   One shared notification system used by all modules. Each user sees their own

   2.5 Reminders
   A pending item waiting for a decision for more than 24 hours triggers a reminder to the
   responsible role [ASSUMPTION: 24h, configurable by admin].



   3. Roles and permissions

   3.1 Admin
        Can do everything and view everything on the factory portal.
        Approves or rejects requests that exceed thresholds.
        Decides outcomes of claims (return, replacement or claim) and may adjust the auto-
        calculated claim amount with a mandatory reason.
        Sets the four thresholds.
        Views analytics (section 7).
        Can intervene on any item, with a reason.


   3.2 Purchase Manager
        Supplier list: view and maintain approved suppliers. Cannot add suppliers (suppliers
        register themselves on the supplier portal and appear here as pending). Can approve
        or reject pending suppliers (reason required on rejection).
        RFQs: post a request for quotation. It goes only to approved suppliers in the relevant
        category.
        Bidding: start bidding, accept a bid, reject a bid (reason required), end bidding.
        Award approval: if the accepted bid’s total value is at or above the purchase threshold,
        the award needs admin approval. The PO is generated only after approval.
        Shipment tracking: track shipments by B/L number using the shipment tracking API
        (key stored server-side).
        Supplier ratings: give ratings to suppliers, maintain supplier profile, review feedback
        and quality results.

        Receives a notification only when a lot is rejected and a claim is raised. Takes no
        decision on claims.


   3.3 Quality Inspector
        Inspect an incoming lot and record accepted quantity and rejected quantity (partial
        acceptance allowed).
        For the accepted quantity: take and upload mandatory photos of the approved lot and
        report to the supervisor.
        For the rejected quantity: highlight the issues (type, severity, description, optional
        photos) and send them to the supervisor.
        Cannot add inventory and cannot override the supervisor.


   3.4 Supervisor
        The only role that adds inventory (raw materials and finished goods). Each inventory
        record stores its type and source (QC-approved lot or production output).
        Reviews inspector reports and accepts or rejects them (rejection requires a reason).
        Reviews issues raised by the inspector: confirm, dismiss (reason required) or
        escalate to admin.
        Answers stock availability queries from Sales.
        Handles stock issues raised by Dispatch (shortage or mismatch): accepts or rejects
        and corrects inventory if needed.
        Inventory adjustments and write-offs require a reason. At or above the inventory
        adjustment threshold, they need admin approval.
        Reports to admin through a structured report (section 7.2).


   3.5 Sales Manager
        Buyer list: buyers register themselves on the buyer portal and appear as pending. The
        sales manager verifies the buyer’s details and approves or rejects (reason on
        rejection). Only approved buyers can transact.
        Buyer RFQs: can respond only to RFQs from approved buyers. RFQs from pending
        buyers are held in state awaiting buyer approval. If the buyer is rejected, their RFQs
        close automatically [ASSUMPTION].
        Quotation: respond to a buyer RFQ with rates, specifications and payment terms.
        Before quoting, may send a stock availability query to the supervisor.

        Sales noting: record the sale once the buyer accepts.
        Auto invoice generation after rates, specs and payment terms are decided.
        Payment tracking: for example 30 days credit. Track the time until due and send alerts
        to the sales manager.
        Dispatch approval: respond to approval requests from Dispatch.
        Sales analytics (section 7).


   3.6 Dispatch
        Track dispatches.
        Request approval from the sales manager before dispatch. Can hold a dispatch on
        failure of certain terms, after a message from the sales manager.
        Raise a stock issue to the supervisor (shortage or mismatch).
        Cannot change invoices, prices or payment terms.



   4. Detailed workflows

   4.1 Supplier approval
    1. Supplier registers on the supplier portal. The record arrives in the factory portal with
        status pending.
    2. Purchase Manager approves or rejects (reason on rejection).
    3. Supplier statuses: pending, approved, rejected, suspended [ASSUMPTION:
        suspended included].
    4. Each supplier has one or more categories (for example yarn, dyes, chemicals,
       packaging). Only approved suppliers receive RFQs.


   4.2 RFQ and bidding
    1. Purchase Manager creates an RFQ (item, specification, quantity, category, required
       date, closing deadline).
    2. On posting, it is sent to all approved suppliers in that category.
    3. Bidding states: draft, open, closed, awarded, cancelled.
    4. Purchase Manager can start bidding, end bidding manually, and bidding also auto-ends
        at the closing deadline.
    5. Purchase Manager accepts or rejects bids (reason on rejection).

    6. Bids are sealed: suppliers cannot see each other’s prices [ASSUMPTION].


   4.3 Award and PO
    1. Purchase Manager accepts a bid.
    2. If total bid value is below the purchase threshold: the PO is generated immediately.
    3. If at or above: status becomes pending admin approval. Admin approves (PO
       generated) or rejects (reason required).
    4. If the PO total later crosses the threshold (for example after a quantity change), it
        needs admin approval again [ASSUMPTION].
    5. The B/L number is attached to the PO and used for shipment tracking.


   4.4 Shipment tracking
        Purchase Manager enters or views the B/L number on the PO and sees status from the
        tracking API.
        If the API fails, show the last known cached status and an error message. Never crash
        the page.


   4.5 Quality inspection and supervisor review
    1. Each lot is linked to its PO, shipment and supplier.
    2. Inspector records accepted quantity and rejected quantity.
    3. Accepted quantity: inspector uploads mandatory photos (timestamped, tied to the lot
        id) and reports to the supervisor.
              Supervisor accepts: the quantity is added to inventory.
              Supervisor rejects (reason required): the report returns to the inspector for re-
              inspection or better photos.
    4. Rejected quantity: inspector raises issues with type and severity. The supervisor
        chooses:
              Confirm: a partial claim is created (4.6).
              Dismiss (reason required): returns to the inspector for re-inspection, or the lot is
              accepted with a noted deviation.
              Escalate to admin: for disputed or high-value cases.
    5. If the confirmed rejection is at or above the claim threshold, it needs admin approval
        before the claim proceeds.

   4.6 Partial claims
    1. Claim amount is calculated automatically: rejected quantity × PO rate for that lot.
    2. Admin decides the outcome: return, replacement or claim.
    3. Admin may adjust the calculated amount. A reason is mandatory. The audit log stores
       the original amount, adjusted amount, reason, admin and timestamp.
    4. The Purchase Manager is notified of the final outcome and amount. No decision by the
        purchase manager.
    5. The outcome feeds the supplier rating.


   4.7 Inventory
        Only the Supervisor adds inventory. Raw material inventory comes only from QC-
        approved lots. Finished goods are added by the supervisor as production output.
        Adjustments and write-offs need a reason and follow the inventory adjustment
        threshold.
        Inventory is deducted at the moment of dispatch.


   4.8 Buyer RFQ to sale to invoice
    1. Buyer registers on the buyer portal (status pending). Sales Manager verifies and
       approves or rejects.
    2. Approved buyer posts an RFQ (the RFQ may also be posted while pending but stays
       locked as awaiting buyer approval).
    3. Sales Manager sends a stock availability query to the Supervisor, who replies with
       available quantity.
    4. Sales Manager responds with a quotation: rates, specifications, payment terms.
    5. When the buyer accepts, Sales Manager notes the sale.
    6. Invoice is generated automatically from the agreed rates, specs and payment terms.
       Once issued, the invoice is locked. Corrections happen through a revised invoice or
        credit note [ASSUMPTION].


   4.9 Payment tracking
        Each invoice has payment terms (for example 30 days credit) and a due date.
        Track payments received, including partial payments [ASSUMPTION].
        Alerts to the Sales Manager at 7 days before due, 3 days before due, on the due date,
        and when overdue [ASSUMPTION: intervals configurable].

        Payment statuses: unpaid, partially paid, paid, overdue.


   4.10 Dispatch
    1. Dispatch statuses: pending approval, approved, on hold, dispatched, delivered.
    2. Dispatch raises a structured approval request tied to the order. The Sales Manager
       approves or holds (hold reason required, for example payment overdue or terms not
        met).
    3. Dispatch can hold a dispatch on failure of terms after a message from the Sales
       Manager. Messaging is a structured request linked to the order, not free-form chat.
    4. If stock is short or mismatched, Dispatch raises a stock issue to the Supervisor. At or
       above the dispatch stock issue threshold, it goes to Admin.
    5. Dispatch record may include vehicle or transport details and delivery proof
        [ASSUMPTION: optional fields].
    6. Inventory is deducted when status becomes dispatched.

   9. Integration with the Supplier and Buyer portals
        Supplier and Buyer portals are separate applications. Do not build them.
        The factory portal receives from them: supplier registrations, supplier bids, buyer
        registrations, buyer RFQs, buyer acceptance of quotations.
        The factory portal sends to them: purchase RFQs, bid results, quotations, invoices.
        Not yet decided: whether these share one database or communicate by API. Ask me
        before building this layer. Until then, build the factory-side data model and endpoints
        so either option can be added.



   10. Out of scope
   Do not build: supplier or buyer portal screens, production planning, payroll or HR,
   manufacturing job tracking, a free-form chat system, payment gateways, or any role
   beyond the six above.



   11. Open items to confirm before building
    1. Invoice fields: tax, currency, numbering format.
    2. Shared database or API between the factory portal and the supplier and buyer portals.
    3. Whether user and role management is in admin scope.
    4. Whether the PO re-approval rule (4.3, point 4) should stay.
    5. Any of the [ASSUMPTION] items above that you want changed.

   Ask me these before starting the parts they affect.
